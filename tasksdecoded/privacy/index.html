TasksDecoded Privacy Policy

Effective October 26, 2026

TasksDecoded is a task, project, goal, and metric tracker for iPhone, iPad, and Mac, made by Wrinkly Brain Studio ("we", "us"). This policy explains what information the app handles. The short version: we don't collect your data. There are no accounts, no ads, and no tracking. The app sends us only anonymous usage counts and crash reports, which you can turn off, and feedback you choose to send.

What stays on your device

Everything you enter (tasks, projects, goals, metrics, notes, lists, settings, and backups you create) is stored on your device. We have no server and cannot see it.

iCloud sync

If you are signed in to iCloud, TasksDecoded syncs your data between your devices using Apple's CloudKit, in your own private iCloud database. That data is stored and protected by Apple under your Apple Account; we cannot access it. You can turn iCloud off for the app in your device's Settings. Apple's handling of iCloud data is described at apple.com/legal/privacy.

Apple Health (optional)

If you link a metric to Apple Health, the app asks permission and reads only the data types you allow, such as steps or weight, to chart them. It never writes to Apple Health, never shares Health data with us or anyone else, and doesn't use it for advertising. Health-derived entries in a metric sync through your private iCloud like your other metrics.

Apple Reminders (optional)

If you turn on Auto Import Reminders, the app asks for access to your reminders. It reads only the reminders in the Reminders lists you choose, adds them to TasksDecoded as tasks, and then deletes them from those lists in Apple Reminders. This all happens on your device; your reminders are never sent to us. Imported tasks sync through your private iCloud like your other tasks. You can turn the feature off in the app's settings at any time, and remove the app's access in your device's Settings under Privacy & Security > Reminders.

Anonymous usage data (optional)

To learn which features help and what gets in the way, the app counts how it is used: how often features are used (for example, "a task was completed"), how long each screen is open, how far the welcome tour gets, and how many items of each kind you have (for example, "7 projects", never what they are), along with the app version, the kind of device, and the system version. Once a day at most, these counts are sent through Apple's CloudKit to the app's own iCloud database, where we view them in summary. They never include anything you write or enter (no titles, notes, names, numbers, or dates), and no name, email, Apple Account, or device identifier. Each copy of the app is known only by a random number it makes for itself, so reports from different days can be counted together; it can't be traced back to you. CloudKit also marks each report with an anonymous ID for the iCloud account that sent it (a random code, not your name, email, or Apple Account), which we use only to count your iPhone, iPad, and Mac as one person. This data isn't linked to you, isn't used for advertising or tracking, and isn't shared with anyone. Reports are sent only when your device is signed in to iCloud. You can turn this off at any time in App Settings > Share Anonymous Usage Data; anything not yet sent is deleted.

Crash reports (optional)

If the app crashes, Apple's MetricKit gives it a technical report of where in the app's code it failed, with the app version, the kind of device, and the system version. While Share Anonymous Usage Data is on, these reports are sent the same way as the usage counts, with the same random number, so we can tell how many devices a problem affects. They contain nothing you've entered. With the setting off, the app asks before sending one.

With the same setting on, the app also sends Apple's daily performance summary (also from MetricKit): how much processor, graphics, network, and storage it used, how long it ran in the background, how quickly it opened, how often it paused or quit unexpectedly, and its memory use. We use it to make the app faster and easier on your battery. It contains nothing you've entered.

Feedback you send

App Settings > Help & Feedback lets you report a bug, suggest a feature, or ask a question. It is sent only when you tap Send, through Apple's CloudKit to the app's own iCloud database. Along with your message, if you leave Include Technical Details on, it carries the app version, your device and system, your language, region, and time zone, iCloud sync status, how many items you have (counts only, never their contents), and a log of recent app events; you can read all of it before sending. Feedback sent this way is anonymous: it doesn't say who sent it, so we can't reply. For a reply, the sheet's Email for a Reply link (and, without iCloud, sending itself) opens your own email app, and we use your address only to answer you. We use feedback only to help you and improve the app, and never share it.

Notifications

Reminders are local notifications created on your device. We don't use push notifications and don't send your reminders anywhere.

Photos and text recognition

If you scan tasks from a photo, the image is read on your device using Apple's on-device frameworks. Photos are not uploaded to us. Features that understand dates or priorities in text also run on your device.

Purchases

Premium is an optional auto-renewing subscription or one-time purchase handled entirely by Apple through the App Store. We never see your payment details. Apple may tell the app whether a subscription is active so features can unlock.

What we do not do

  • We do not collect, sell, rent, or share personal information.
  • We do not use advertising or tracking, and have no third-party SDKs that collect data. The only analytics are the anonymous usage counts, crash reports, and performance summaries described above.
  • We do not knowingly collect information from children.

Your control

You can export your data or delete it from Settings in the app at any time. Deleting the app removes the data on that device; data synced to iCloud can be removed in your iCloud storage settings.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update the date above and, for significant changes, mention it in the app's release notes.
